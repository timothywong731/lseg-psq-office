# Measured Light

The conceptual seed is the exchange between movement and stillness: a building for rapidly moving information held inside a disciplined stone-and-glass frame. Repeated architectural bays establish a quiet spatial rhythm. A single rotated cube interrupts that rhythm at human scale, giving the larger volume its centre of gravity.

The model grows from a small set of geometric relationships: bay spacing, floor height, void width and stair rise. Mullions, fascia, lights and furniture inherit these relationships rather than being placed independently. This meticulously crafted algorithm treats alignment as its primary material; consistency across hundreds of elements makes the space legible from any viewpoint.

Seeded variation belongs only in secondary details. Stone shades vary slightly within a controlled palette, and furnishings repeat with restrained offsets. The architecture remains fixed when the seed changes. The aim is a master-level implementation whose computational care is visible in the absence of noise, with painstaking optimization joining repeated geometry into a small number of material batches.

Light provides temporal variation. A continuous daylight parameter changes the balance of cool skylight and warm interior illumination, while the camera moves between three measured compositions. The state evolves smoothly but never randomly: the same model and settings always produce the same composition. Reflections and translucent panes reveal the tension between enclosure and openness.

This is an architectural interpretation, not a literal p5.js artwork. The algorithmic-art skill's seeded process and interactive exploration are expressed in Blender geometry and a Three.js viewer as requested. The final craft is judged by recognisable spatial relationships, navigable views and efficient rendering, not decorative complexity.
